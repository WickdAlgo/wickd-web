# Sprint 2: 2026-08-01

## Sprint Goal

Turn `/platform` from a demonstration into the frontend host for the Wickd
platform: addressable routes, versioned contracts behind a gateway, a real
chart renderer, and causal replay proven end to end on one inspection run and
one trade.

## Product Alignment

- Executes phases W0–W5 of
  `Wickd_Web_Integrated_Trade_Journal_Handoff.md`, which decides the trade
  journal is a platform module rather than a standalone application or part of
  `Wickd.Core`.
- Opens `docs/releases/v0.2.0.md`.
- Amends `PRODUCT.md`, whose Boundaries section currently forbids what this
  sprint builds toward. That amendment is Item 1, not a side effect.
- Leaves `docs/releases/v0.1.0.md` closable: nothing here changes the marketing
  surface.

## Dates

- **Start:** 2026-08-01
- **End:** 2026-08-08

## Capacity And Assumptions

- Single maintainer with agent assistance.
- **This sprint shares Sprint 1's window.** Sprint 1 was documentation-only and
  closed on its opening day with its Review and Retro filled, so Sprint 2 opens
  immediately rather than leaving the week idle. The overlap is deliberate, not
  a bookkeeping error.
- **Seven delivery items in one week is an overcommitment**, and it was
  committed to knowingly. Items 1–4 are independently deployable and land
  first. If the window closes early, the carryover order is Item 5, then 6,
  then 7 — recorded here in advance so the decision is not made under
  pressure at review time. Item 8 is unplanned workflow maintenance and does
  not alter that order.
- Two runtime dependencies enter a repository that has had three since it was
  created: `lightweight-charts` and `zod`. Bundle impact is a tracked risk, not
  an assumption.
- No backend exists and none is built. Every contract is provisional and every
  payload is a fixture. `Wickd.Inspection` will later be canonical, so the
  contracts sit behind one adapter boundary.
- Every `main` commit deploys. Everything here reaches production when the
  `stage` to `main` promotion lands, including the journal.

## Retro

- **Keep:** Writing the fixture so its structures are *found in* the data rather
  than asserted beside it. Every serious defect this sprint was a claim the data
  did not support, and the fixture tests are what turned each one from a
  rendering mystery into a failing assertion.
- **Keep:** Pulling the unit runner forward to Item 3 instead of leaving it at
  Item 7. The contracts and the replay filters are the parts that can be wrong
  invisibly; building them without a way to run them would have deferred every
  discovery to browser inspection.
- **Change:** Four of the chart bugs were invisible to the test suite by
  construction — the projection measured the wrong element, the overlays were
  painted over by a canvas, zones hid the price action, a clipped line had the
  wrong slope. Each *rendered something*, which is why they survived unit tests
  and died in a browser within seconds. Look at the thing earlier; a passing
  suite is evidence about logic, not about pixels.
- **Change:** The estimate was wrong in an instructive direction. Seven items
  did fit, but only because five of them were mechanical once the contracts
  existed. The unpredictable work was all in the fixture and the chart — the
  places where the data and the renderer had to agree with each other.
- **Follow up:** The outcome leak found in Item 6 — replaying an entry while
  showing its net R — is the kind of error that would have shipped unnoticed.
  Worth a deliberate pass over anything else that reveals a result before the
  cursor reaches it.
